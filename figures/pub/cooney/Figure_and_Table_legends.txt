LEGENDS: FIGURES AND TABLES
Avian plumage colour and land-use change (Cooney ultraviolet-sensitive metrics)

Figures 1 and 2 are introductory and are prepared outside this repository.
Legends below cover the three figures and two tables built by
scripts/cooney/pub_figures.R and the two post-hoc scripts, followed by the
supplementary figures and tables (Tables S1-S3 built by scripts/cooney/supp_tables.R).


FIGURE 3. Species-level colour against land-use association (model A1b).

Each species' association score is the proportion of its detection records
falling in a given land use, so the five scores sum to one. (a, d) Male
colourfulness and sexual dichromatism mapped onto 1,375 tips of the
BigBirdTree phylogeny by ancestral reconstruction. Scales are clipped to the
2nd and 98th percentiles for colourfulness, and to the 10th and 90th
percentile magnitude for dichromatism, which is signed. (b, e) Points are
means of successive deciles of the association score within each land use,
with 95% confidence intervals, excluding species scoring zero. Lines are
posterior medians from the compositional model, solid across the observed
range of scores and dotted beyond it. (c, f) Pairwise contrasts between land
uses, from posterior draws, with 95% credible intervals. Filled symbols mark
intervals excluding zero. n = 1,242 species.


FIGURE 5. Colour and abundance change after land-use conversion (model A2d).

The response is a species' relative abundance at a modified site minus its own
mean in primary vegetation in the same study, so primary vegetation is
absorbed into the baseline and four land uses remain. (a to d) Partial
residuals, with the study mean and the species mean both removed, because
colour is constant within a species and the raw margin reflects which studies
sampled which species. Points are means of twelfths of the records with 95%
confidence intervals, and the grey band is a linear fit. (e) Observed values by biome and land use, colour ignored.
Boxes give quartiles, whiskers 1.5 times the interquartile range, outliers
omitted. (f, g) Posterior contrasts and colour slopes with 95% credible
intervals. One study supplying 94% of the tropical open cropland cell is
excluded throughout. n = 50,534 differences.


FIGURE 6. Community plumage colour across biomes and land uses (model A3b).

Community values are taken over records of species detected at a site, with
body mass and trophic niche entering as covariates. (a, c) Observed male
colourfulness, on a log scale, and sexual dichromatism, by land use and
biome. Boxes give quartiles and whiskers 1.5 times the interquartile range.
Outliers are omitted and each axis is set to the whisker range. (b, d)
Posterior contrasts with 95% credible intervals, grouped into blocks by
biome. Within a block every row is that land use against primary vegetation
in the same biome, so rows are not comparable across blocks. The final block
holds the body-mass slope per standard deviation rather than a contrast.
Filled symbols mark intervals excluding zero, open symbols those including
it. n = 24,614 records.


TABLE 1. Post-hoc contrasts, main effects.

Every row is a posterior contrast rather than a model coefficient. Contrasts
are formed by taking posterior draws of the linear predictor with random
effects excluded, averaging over a balanced grid of the remaining factors,
and differencing draw by draw. Reported are the posterior median, the 95%
credible interval, the posterior probability that the difference takes its
estimated sign, and a standardised effect size, the estimate divided by the
residual standard deviation of that response on the scale the model works in.
No multiplicity correction is applied, because these are posterior
probabilities of a difference rather than repeated frequentist tests. The
table carries one model per analysis: A3b for community colour, A2c for
relative abundance, A2d for abundance change, and A1b for the species-level
regression.


TABLE 2. Post-hoc contrasts within levels of an interacting factor.

Table 1 averages over the factor a term interacts with, which can cancel
opposing effects. Table 2 recomputes the same contrasts separately within
each level of that factor, which is what an interaction requires. Rows are of
two kinds, named in the quantity column: level means, comparing two land uses
or two biomes while the other factor is held at a stated level, and colour
slopes, giving the change per standard deviation of a colour metric within a
stated level. Columns match Table 1. All 284 rows are reconstructed from the
fitted coefficients as a check that none is missing and none depends on a term
the models do not contain, including any three-way colour by biome by land-use
term, which no reported model carries.


SUPPLEMENTARY FIGURES
(built by scripts/cooney/supp_fig_S1_tree.R, C1_sample_bias.R and
supp_fig_S3_S4.R; each colour is a separate variable, the proportion of the
male plumage in that colour, from Delhey et al. 2023, and no ratios are formed)


FIGURE S1. Plumage colour across the passerine phylogeny.

The BigBirdTree phylogeny pruned to the 4,118 passerines with Delhey et al.
(2023) colour data. Rings from inner to outer: (1) species present in the
PREDICTS data; (2) male colourfulness (Cooney et al. 2022, log scale, clipped
to the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles); (3) sexual dichromatism, the plate score
of Cooney et al. (2022), clipped to -1 and 1; (4 to 13) the proportion of the
male plumage in each of ten colours, shaded from white (none) to the full
colour at the 97.5th percentile of that colour across passerines. Light grey
cells mark species without the measurement.


FIGURE S2. Sampling bias in plumage colour.

Cumulative distributions of each colour metric for passerines in the PREDICTS
data (blue) and all other passerines measured by the source data set (grey):
male colourfulness (log10 scale) and sexual dichromatism (1,542 and 1,519
sampled species against 2,985 and 2,896 others; Cooney et al. 2022) and the ten
colour proportions (1,655 against 3,792; Delhey et al. 2023). Each panel gives
the difference between sampled and other species estimated by phylogenetic
generalised least squares with Pagel's lambda (PGLS), and its p-value. No
metric differed credibly after accounting for phylogeny (all p > 0.1); the
differences visible in some raw distributions reflect the clades sampled.


FIGURE S3. Multi-response phylogenetic mixed model of colour and land use.

Eight colour proportions and four land-use association scores were fitted
jointly as responses (MCMCglmm; Halliwell et al. 2025), each on its own logit
scale and standardised, for 1,313 species. Grey and purple, and primary
vegetation, were omitted, because each set of proportions sums to one and
purple is almost absent from most species. (a) Posterior median correlations,
phylogenetic below the diagonal and residual (non-phylogenetic) above it.
Outlined cells have 95% credible intervals excluding zero. (b) Phylogenetic
signal of each response, the phylogenetic share of its variance, with 95%
credible intervals.


FIGURE S4. Land-use effects on each colour.

(a) Multi-response model (Figure S3): conditional slope of each colour on each
land-use score at the species level (phylogenetic plus residual covariance),
in standard deviations, holding the other three scores constant, so that
primary vegetation is the reference. (b) Abundance-change model (A2d) fitted
once per colour: slope of abundance change on the standardised colour
proportion, overall and within each land use, with random effects excluded.
Lines are 95% credible intervals; filled symbols mark intervals excluding zero.
In (b) the axis is truncated; green intervals extend beyond it (arrows) because
its land-use slopes average over biomes and carry an imprecise temperate closed
slope estimated from few green species.


TABLE S1. Male versus sex-averaged colourfulness.

Every colourfulness quantity reported from the species-level (A1b), abundance-change
(A2d) and assemblage-level (A3b) models, estimated once with male colourfulness and once
with the mean of male and female colourfulness (Cooney et al. 2022). The second fit of
each model uses the same records, formula, priors and sampler. Estimates are posterior
medians with 95% credible intervals, on the scale of each model as in Tables 1 and 2;
same_conclusion marks rows where the interval excludes zero under both metrics with the
same sign, or under neither.


TABLE S2. Abundance change and specific colours.

The abundance-change model (A2d) fitted once per colour, with the z-standardised
proportion of the male plumage in that colour (Delhey et al. 2023) in place of
colourfulness. Slopes are the change in abundance change per standard deviation of the
colour proportion, overall and within each land use and biome, with random effects
excluded. pd is the posterior probability of the sign of the estimate, and effect_size
the slope divided by the standard deviation of the response. The last two columns give
the maximum Rhat and minimum bulk effective sample size of each fit. n = 50,230 records
from 1,099 species.


TABLE S3. Results under two phylogenies.

Every analysis that includes the phylogeny was repeated with the maximum clade
credibility tree of 1,000 posterior trees of Jetz et al. (2012; Hackett backbone) in
place of the maximum-likelihood time tree of Claramunt et al. (2025), with identical
data and species. Each row gives one reported quantity: the posterior median with its
95% credible interval under each tree. For the sample-bias test (C1), the estimate is the
PGLS difference between the species in the data and other passerines, and "credible"
means p < 0.05. same_conclusion marks rows where the interval excludes zero under both
trees with the same sign, or under neither. 509 of 550 quantities agree; in each of
the others, one bound of the interval under at least one tree lies close to zero. Of the results
reported in the main text, two are not credible with the Jetz et al. (2012) tree: the
higher proportion of black plumage in species associated with pastures (multi-response
model) and the smaller losses of white-plumaged species in croplands (abundance change).


TABLE S4. Assemblage colour and specific colours.

Difference between each land-use type and primary vegetation in the proportion of the
male plumage in each colour across the species recorded at a site, within each biome,
from the assemblage-level model (A3b) fitted once per colour (23,407 records, 1,254
species; results/cooney/A3b_colours_contrasts.csv). Standard deviations of the
logit-transformed species-level colour proportion. For rufous, red, green and blue a
dagger marks credible contrasts reversed in a presence (Bernoulli) model. Intervals are
likely too narrow because each species is repeated at every site where it was detected.
