LEGENDS: FIGURES AND TABLES
Avian plumage colour and land-use change (Cooney ultraviolet-sensitive metrics)

Figures 1 and 2 are introductory and are prepared outside this repository.
Legends below cover the three figures and two tables built by
scripts/cooney/pub_figures.R and the two post-hoc scripts.


FIGURE 3. Species-level colour against land-use association (model A1b).

Each species' association score is the proportion of its detection records
falling in a given land use, so the five scores sum to one. (a, d) Male
colourfulness and sexual dichromatism mapped onto 1,375 tips of the
BigBirdTree phylogeny by ancestral reconstruction. Scales are clipped to the
2nd and 98th percentiles for colourfulness, and to the 10th and 90th
percentile magnitude for dichromatism, which is signed. (b, e) Points are
means of successive deciles of the association score within each land use,
with 95% confidence intervals, excluding species scoring zero. Lines are
posterior medians from the compositional model, solid across the observed
range of scores and dotted beyond it. (c, f) Pairwise contrasts between land
uses, from posterior draws, with 95% credible intervals. Filled symbols mark
intervals excluding zero. n = 1,242 species.


FIGURE 5. Colour and abundance change after land-use conversion (model A2d).

The response is a species' relative abundance at a modified site minus its own
mean in primary vegetation in the same study, so primary vegetation is
absorbed into the baseline and four land uses remain. (a to d) Partial
residuals, with the study mean and the species mean both removed, because
colour is constant within a species and the raw margin reflects which studies
sampled which species. Points are means of twelfths of the records with 95%
confidence intervals, and the grey band is a linear fit. (e) Observed values by biome and land use, colour ignored.
Boxes give quartiles, whiskers 1.5 times the interquartile range, outliers
omitted. (f, g) Posterior contrasts and colour slopes with 95% credible
intervals. One study supplying 94% of the tropical open cropland cell is
excluded throughout. n = 50,534 differences.


FIGURE 6. Community plumage colour across biomes and land uses (model A3b).

Community values are taken over records of species detected at a site, with
body mass and trophic niche entering as covariates. (a, c) Observed male
colourfulness, on a log scale, and sexual dichromatism, by land use and
biome. Boxes give quartiles and whiskers 1.5 times the interquartile range.
Outliers are omitted and each axis is set to the whisker range. (b, d)
Posterior contrasts with 95% credible intervals, grouped into blocks by
biome. Within a block every row is that land use against primary vegetation
in the same biome, so rows are not comparable across blocks. The final block
holds the body-mass slope per standard deviation rather than a contrast.
Filled symbols mark intervals excluding zero, open symbols those including
it. n = 24,614 records.


TABLE 1. Post-hoc contrasts, main effects.

Every row is a posterior contrast rather than a model coefficient. Contrasts
are formed by taking posterior draws of the linear predictor with random
effects excluded, averaging over a balanced grid of the remaining factors,
and differencing draw by draw. Reported are the posterior median, the 95%
credible interval, the posterior probability that the difference takes its
estimated sign, and a standardised effect size, the estimate divided by the
residual standard deviation of that response on the scale the model works in.
No multiplicity correction is applied, because these are posterior
probabilities of a difference rather than repeated frequentist tests. Rows
labelled A1 come from the superseded species-level model, whose association
scores counted every raw record including structural zeros, and are retained
so the sensitivity check against A1b stays auditable.


TABLE 2. Post-hoc contrasts within levels of an interacting factor.

Table 1 averages over the factor a term interacts with, which can cancel
opposing effects. Table 2 recomputes the same contrasts separately within
each level of that factor, which is what an interaction requires. Rows are of
two kinds, named in the quantity column: level means, comparing two land uses
or two biomes while the other factor is held at a stated level, and colour
slopes, giving the change per standard deviation of a colour metric within a
stated level. Columns match Table 1. All 284 rows are reconstructed from the
fitted coefficients as a check that none is missing and none depends on a term
the models do not contain, including any three-way colour by biome by land-use
term, which no reported model carries.
